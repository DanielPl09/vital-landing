Intake forms rarely fail because a patient refuses to fill them in. They fail because the patient stalls — and every question that doesn't apply to them is one more reason to close the tab.

So we stopped asking those questions.

## Branch between assessments, not inside them

Vital runs the same validated instruments your profession already trusts. What it changes is *which* ones a given patient ever sees.

Someone with no sleep complaint never opens a sleep battery. Someone whose answers point at disordered eating gets that screener, in full. The questionnaire decides at the door — not question by question.

That distinction matters more than it sounds. A PSQI global score needs all nineteen of its items; drop a few and what comes back isn't a PSQI any more, it's just a number. So Vital never branches *inside* an instrument. It branches between them.

You get a shorter intake in which every score is still the score it was validated to be.

## Most answers should be a tap

Typing is the slowest thing a patient does on a phone and the quickest way to lose them.

Wherever an answer has a known shape — a frequency, a range, a yes — Vital offers it as a ready button. The patient taps, the record fills, and nobody has to spell "occasionally" while standing on a bus.

The buttons are not cosmetic. They are what makes the difference between an intake a patient finishes in one sitting and one they promise to come back to.

## Tuned to the people who actually answer it

A question set that reads well in one language and one health system reads badly in another. Phrasing, units, portion sizes, the way a frequency is described in ordinary speech — all of it shifts.

So the branching logic and the ready answers are tuned per locale rather than translated once and shipped everywhere. What a patient taps should sound like something they would have said.

## Verified before it reaches a clinic

Every change to the branching is rehearsed against simulated patients drawn from published population data, segment by segment, before any of it is offered to a real person.
